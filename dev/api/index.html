The deterministic block

RigorousUlam2D.UlamTree — Type
UlamTree(F, domain, d0, d1; bc=:periodic, enclosure_method=first_order_enclosure,
         area=nothing, store=mktempdir())

The refinement of the file docstring as an object kept on disk: refine!(tree) descends one level, tree.level is the depth of the coarsest uncertain cell, and ulam_matrices(tree, d) reads the graph and the matrices at any depth d0 ≤ d ≤ min(tree.level, d1), so a long refinement hands out the coarse matrices as it goes and each level halves their widths.

The tree is split into one subtree per cell of depth d0, each in its own file root<k>.jls of the directory store, written whole after every refinement of that subtree; only the subtrees being refined are in memory. Constructing a tree on a store that already holds one with the same domain, depths and boundary condition resumes it, so an interrupted refinement is continued by running it again; F and enclosure_method are not stored and must be the ones the store was built with. ulam_hierarchy is the one-shot form.

area(box, W), if given, encloses the fraction of the box box that F maps into the window W, both IntervalBox{2}, with no boundary condition applied; ulam_matrices then reads the uncertain leaves through it (see the file docstring and fibred_area).

source
RigorousUlam2D.refine! — Function
refine!(tree::UlamTree; targets=nothing) -> tree

Quadrisect the uncertain cells, every subtree in turn: the candidates of a child are the cells met by the enclosure of its image among the children of its parent's candidates (above d1), or among its parent's candidates (at or below d1); a child with a single candidate at depth d1 or finer is resolved. With targets, a vector of booleans indexed as build_uniform_partition(domain, d1), only the cells at depth d1 or finer having a candidate among the marked targets are quadrisected, which refines the rows of U that are too heavy; cells coarser than d1 are always quadrisected.

source
RigorousUlam2D.repair! — Function
repair!(tree::UlamTree; level=tree.level) -> Vector{Int}

Read every subtree file and rebuild those that cannot be read or are not well formed, from their cell of depth d0 refined uniformly to the depth level; returns the roots rebuilt. A subtree is written whole to a temporary file and renamed, but a file system may still lose the data of a renamed file when the disk fills during its writeback, which is how a truncated subtree arises.

source
RigorousUlam2D.ulam_matrices — Function
ulam_matrices(tree::UlamTree, d; level=0) -> (depth, partition, graph, L_lo, U)
ulam_matrices(tree::UlamTree, depths::AbstractRange; level=0) -> Vector of the above

The graph and the Ulam matrix enclosure at depth d, d0 ≤ d ≤ min(tree.level, d1), read off the subtrees: every cell, resolved or uncertain, contributes its mass 4^{-(ℓ-d)} relative to its ancestor at depth d to L_lo when the ancestors at depth d of its candidates are a single cell and to U for each of them otherwise. Matrices are indexed as partition = build_uniform_partition(domain, d). Several depths are read in one pass over the subtrees.

With level finer than tree.level, the uncertain cells are quadrisected in memory, depth first and cell by cell, down to the depth level before being read, and nothing is written to the store: the matrices are those refine! would give after the same quadrisections, at the memory cost of one descent, and d may then reach min(level, d1). level may also be a vector of integers indexed as build_uniform_partition(domain, d1), the depth asked for the cells feeding each row: a cell at depth d1 or finer is quadrisected while one of its candidates asks for a finer depth, so a row that is too heavy is refined further than the others, and repeated calls that raise the levels of the heavy rows only keep what the previous calls gave the rest.

source
RigorousUlam2D.ulam_hierarchy — Function
ulam_hierarchy(F, domain, d0, d1; leaf_depth=8, bc=:periodic,
               enclosure_method=first_order_enclosure, area=nothing, store=mktempdir())

Transition graphs and Ulam matrix enclosures at the depths d0:d1 (see the file docstring): a UlamTree refined to depth d1 + leaf_depth, read at each depth with ulam_matrices. Returns a vector with one named tuple (depth, partition, graph, L_lo, U) per depth. The uncertain mass of a cell at depth d is at most the mass of a strip of width diam(domain) 2^{-(d1 + leaf_depth)} around the preimages of the grid lines of depth d1.

RigorousUlam2D.fibred_area — Function
fibred_area(xonly, xcomp, yof; yint=nothing, xinv=nothing, bisect=12) -> area

An area function for UlamTree, for a map F(x, y) whose component xonly (1 or 2) depends on x alone, xcomp(x), and whose other component is strictly monotone in y for every fixed x, with yof(x, w) the y at which it equals w, yint(x, δ, w) the integral of yof(·, w) from x to x + δ, and xinv(X, w) an interval holding every x in X at which xcomp(x) = w (empty or wider when convenient). The four are interval extensions: xcomp and yof take an interval x, yint thin x and δ, xinv takes the interval X (to choose a branch of the inverse) and w is always thin. yint is to be written as an increment, not as the difference of an antiderivative at the two ends: the pieces are as small as 2^-bisect of a leaf, and the difference of two values of the antiderivative loses the integral of such a piece in their rounding (for a polynomial antiderivative, expand in δ; for 1/x², write the difference over a common denominator).

The fraction of a box [x0, x1] × [y0, y1] that F maps into a window W is the integral over x of the length of [y0, y1] ∩ {y : F(x, y) ∈ W}. The part of [x0, x1] that xcomp sends into the window's xonly side is found by bisection, and on a piece inside it the length is the difference of the two inverses yof(x, w), w the ends of the other side, each clamped to [y0, y1]: the integral of a clamped inverse over a piece is the piece's width times the clamp value where the inverse lies beyond it, and yint over the piece where it lies within [y0, y1], intersected with the piece's width times the range of the inverse. A piece on which xcomp crosses the window's end, the inverse crosses y0 or y1, or the monotonicity of F in y cannot be told, is bisected, up to bisect times from the whole box, and is then bounded by its width times the range of the integrand over it. Without yint every piece is bounded that way, which makes the enclosure a Riemann sum over 2^bisect pieces.

With xinv a piece on which xcomp crosses the window's end is cut instead of bisected: the crossings with the two ends are enclosed by xinv, and between them, xcomp being continuous, the piece lies within or outside the window throughout, which is read at one point; the crossing enclosures are bounded by their width times the range of the integrand. The residual of such a crossing is then the width of xinv's enclosure in place of 2^-bisect of the box, and the box is bisected only where the inverse crosses y0 or y1 (where a piece cannot be read, it is bisected as without xinv).

RigorousUlam2D.metzger_lorenz_system — Function
metzger_lorenz_system(; ρ = 18//5, bisect = 30, xinv = true) -> (; name, F, domain, area)

The contracting Lorenz map of the paper's third example (Section 6.3), on [-1/2, 1/2]², periodic: for x > 0, F = (ρ x² − 1/2, y x⁶ − 1/4), for x < 0, F = (1/2 − ρ x², −y x⁶ + 1/4). The first component ℓ is increasing on both branches, ℓ′(x) = 2ρ|x|, so K₂|x|^{s−1} ≤ ℓ′ ≤ K₁|x|^{s−1} with s = 2, its Schwarzian derivative −(3/2)(ℓ″/ℓ′)² is negative, and ℓ(0±) = ∓1/2: condition (A0) of Metzger, Ann. IHP 17 (2000) 247, p. 249; ρ ≤ 4 keeps ℓ in [-1/2, 1/2]. The fibre contracts like |x|^r with r = 6. ρ is a rational, entered as an interval quotient; the name carries it (mlorenz_rho<p>-<q>).

The crossings of ℓ = w are x = ((w + 1/2)/ρ)^{1/2} (x > 0) and x = −((1/2 − w)/ρ)^{1/2} (x < 0), empty where the radicand is negative. The fibre inverse is (w + 1/4)/x⁶ (x > 0) and (1/4 − w)/x⁶ (x < 0), and its integral over [x, x + δ] on pieces of one sign is that factor times ((x + δ)⁵ − x⁵)/(5 x⁵ (x + δ)⁵), written as δ(5x⁴ + 10x³δ + 10x²δ² + 5xδ³ + δ⁴) over the same denominator, as fibred_area asks.

RigorousUlam2D.SquareNoise — Type

The Ulam projection of the uniform noise on [-a[1], a[1]] × [-a[2], a[2]] (a number a is the same half-width on both axes) on the grid of depth depth of domain, with the boundary condition bc, as an operator applied by apply! or * to vectors in grid order. RigorousUlam2D.norms_of_powers_columns — Function
norms_of_powers_columns(blockmul!, n, m; η1, eta1A = 0.0, normP = 1.0, u,
                        block = 64, verbose = false) -> C

Upper bounds C[j] ≥ ‖P^j|_{V_h}‖₁, j = 1, …, m, where V_h = {v ∈ ℝⁿ : Σ vᵢ = 0}, for a linear operator P known through a computed product that obeys, column by column,

‖fl(P y) − P y‖₁ ≤ η1 ‖y‖₁ + eta1A,      ‖P‖₁ ≤ normP.

The bound. For v ∈ V_h and any fixed u ∈ ℝⁿ, v = Σᵢ vᵢ (eᵢ − u) because Σᵢ vᵢ = 0, so P^j v = Σᵢ vᵢ (P^j eᵢ − u) and

‖P^j|_{V_h}‖₁ ≤ maxᵢ ‖P^j eᵢ − u‖₁.

No property of P beyond linearity is used; u is a free choice, and near the fixed point of a mixing P it makes the bound decay with j.

The rounding. Each column is iterated in floating point from x₀ = eᵢ, exact; with ε_j ≥ ‖x_j − P^j eᵢ‖₁, ε₀ = 0, and x_{j+1} − P^{j+1} eᵢ = (fl(P x_j) − P x_j) + P (x_j − P^j eᵢ),

ε_{j+1} = η1 ‖x_j‖₁ + eta1A + normP ε_j,

so C[j] = maxᵢ (‖x_j − u‖₁ + ε_j), every operation rounded upward.

blockmul!(Y, X) must set every column of Y to the computed product of P with the same column of X (n × b matrices), under the model above; it is called on disjoint blocks of block columns from several threads at once, so it must be thread safe. Cost: n m column products.

With cols, only the columns i ∈ cols are iterated and C[j] is the maximum over them; the entrywise maximum of the results for ranges covering 1:n is the C of the whole run, bit for bit, so a sweep can be split between machines (bench/l1_mixing.jl with COLS, bench/l1_mixing_merge.jl).

source
RigorousUlam2D.norms_of_powers_sparse — Function
norms_of_powers_sparse(P, m; rigorous = true, u = nothing, block = 64, verbose = false) -> C

norms_of_powers_columns for an explicit matrix P, taken as the operator itself. With w the largest number of nonzeros in a row or a column of P, an entry of P y is an inner product of at most w terms, so |fl(P y) − P y| ≤ γ_w |P| |y| + (underflow) entrywise [Higham, Accuracy and Stability of Numerical Algorithms, 2nd ed., (3.11)]; whence η1 = γ_w ‖|P|‖₁, eta1A = √n η_A with the underflow term η_A of _eta_stored for w products, and normP = ‖|P|‖₁, all upward. rigorous = false sets η1 = eta1A = 0. u defaults to the power iteration ulam_fixed_point of P.

RigorousUlam2D.coordinate_variations — Function
coordinate_variations(m, k, h) -> (; var1, var2)

Upper bounds on var₁(f) and var₂(f), the variations in the directions e₁ and e₂ on the torus of the function f constant on the cells with masses m (grid order, k × k cells of side h, M[iy, ix]): the jump |m_i − m_j| / h² across an edge of length h, summed over the k² edges normal to e₁ (between horizontal neighbours) for var₁ and over those normal to e₂ for var₂, every operation upward. var₁₂(f) = var₁(f) + var₂(f) is what Theorem C (item 1) uses; the isotropic variation Var(f) is not computed here.

source
RigorousUlam2D.x_marginal — Function
x_marginal(m, k, h) -> Vector{Float64}

The density of the first marginal of the function constant on the cells with masses m (grid order, k × k cells of side h, M[iy, ix]): entry ix is the mass of column ix divided by its width h. Integrating out y is a contraction of L¹, so a bound on ‖f − f̃‖₁ bounds the L¹ distance of the marginals. The sums are in floating point: a value for display, not a bound.

RigorousUlam2D.sigma_from_norms — Function
sigma_from_norms(C) -> (; grid, V, k, α)

Σ_{i<k} min(1, C_i) / (1 − α), α = C_k, C_0 = 1, over the k with C_k < 1, at the k giving the smallest value (grid), and V = 1 + grid, for bounds C[i] ≥ ‖P_h^i|_{V_h}‖₁, i = 1, …, length(C): on V_h, Σ_{j ≥ 0} P_h^j = Σ_{m ≥ 0} P_h^{mk} Σ_{i < k} P_h^i and ‖P_h^{mk}|_{V_h}‖ ≤ αᵐ, and C_i ≤ 1 may be used for every i since P_h is a Markov operator; see the file docstring. Upward.

RigorousUlam2D.coarse_fine_norms — Function
coarse_fine_norms(C, h, var12ρ; first = 1.0) -> F

Bounds F_k ≥ ‖Λ^k|_V‖_{L¹}, k = 1, …, length(C) + 1, for Λ = σ N_ξ σ L_T σ with σ the Ulam projection of any dyadic refinement of the grid of squares of side h, or the identity (Λ = L_ξ), from bounds C_k ≥ ‖P_h^k|_{V_h}‖₁ for the coarse P_h = π_h N_ξ π_h L_T π_h (norms_of_powers_columns; they bound ‖P_h^k|_V‖₁ as well, P_h ending with π_h). RigorousUlam2D.swapped_error — Function
swapped_error(Σ, h, var12ρ, ε1, ε2) -> bound

‖f_ξ − f̃‖₁ for a computed f̃ ≥ 0 that is constant on the cells of the grid of squares of side h of P_h, with ε1 ≥ ‖P_h f̃ − f̃‖₁, ε2 ≥ |Σf̃ − 1| (fixed_point_residual) and Σ ≥ ‖(I − L_ξ)⁻¹|_V‖₁ (sigma_from_norms of coarse_fine_norms, which bound the powers of L_ξ itself). With u = f̃/Σf̃, u − f_ξ ∈ V and

(I − L_ξ)(u − f_ξ) = (u − P_h u) + (P_h − L_ξ) u;

in L_ξ − P_h = (I − π_h) N_ξ L + π_h N_ξ (I − π_h) L + π_h N_ξ π_h L (I − π_h) the last term vanishes on u, which π_h fixes, and the other two are at most θ_h each (‖u‖₁ = 1; file docstring). Hence, as in prop:l1error with θ₁ = θ₂ = θ_h,

‖u − f_ξ‖₁ ≤ Σ (h var₁₂(ρ_ξ) + ε1/(1 − ε2)),   ‖f̃ − u‖₁ = |Σf̃ − 1| ≤ ε2,

and the bound is their sum, rounded upward; against 3Σθ_h of the direct route it spends 2θ_h.
